Launch angle is the vertical direction the ball travels immediately after contact — negative means it's headed into the ground, positive means it's on a line-drive or fly-ball trajectory (general definition). These coaches make the point that launch angle is a *result*, not something you should directly manipulate — it's feedback about how the swing and contact point actually worked, not a command to "lift every swing" [Attack Angle | Allow launch angle to be the result @ n/a]. They also emphasize that good hitters consistently produce positive launch angles, and specifically that ~20-25 degrees combined with high exit velocity tends to produce home runs, while around 15 degrees still produces good line drives [Episode 8- Launch Angle | What is Launch Angle? @ 0:00].

To improve launch angle, Gradum teaches that you shouldn't chase it by dropping the back shoulder, lowering the hands, leaning back, or scooping under the ball — those moves just manufacture a weak pop-up rather than a genuinely useful positive angle [Attack Angle | Another Wrong Approach: Chasing Launch Angle @ 0:00]. Instead, positive launch angle should come from creating backspin by swinging "to and through" the baseball, rather than trying to create backspin by swinging down on it — swinging down creates too much spin and just pops the ball up [Episode 8- Launch Angle | How to create a positive Launch Angle? @ 0:00]. The coaches also stress that attack angle (the bat's direction) and launch angle (the ball's direction) are related but separate — a positive attack angle only helps if you also square up the ball, since contact above center produces groundballs, centered contact produces hard low line drives, slightly below center produces lift, and too far below center produces pop-ups [Attack Angle | Attack Angle Versus Launch Angle @ 0:00]. Practically, this means starting from a balanced position, loading without dropping the barrel underneath the pitch, and letting the positive attack angle develop from body rotation and barrel turn rather than an artificial scoop [Attack Angle | Step-by-Step Explanation @ 0:00].

If a swing produces a groundball, these coaches recommend diagnosing why (chopped down, hit the top of the ball, mistimed) rather than just swinging higher next time [Attack Angle | Step-by-Step Explanation > Allow launch angle to be the result @ 0:00].
