## Creating Separation for a Smooth Barrel Turn
Creating separation helps a hitter keep the hands connected, adjust to different pitch speeds, and deliver the barrel through a long hitting zone. This can produce hard line drives and home runs.

The Roll teaches this movement with a basketball. The basketball provides immediate feedback because its size makes it easy to feel when the hands or back elbow move out of position.

## What is Separation?
Separation occurs when the lower body begins moving toward the pitcher while the upper body remains loaded.

During the stride:
- The lower body gains ground.
- The rear hip remains loaded.
- The rear shoulder blade, or scapula, stays engaged.
- The hands remain near the rear shoulder.
- The upper body does not drift forward with the stride.

Separation does not mean pulling the hands as far behind the body as possible. Excessive stretching creates tension and can disconnect the arms from the torso.

The objective is to move forward with the lower body while keeping the upper body organized and ready to swing.
## The Roll Drill
### Purpose of the Drill

The Roll Drill teaches the hitter to:
- Create separation during the stride.
- Land without committing the hands too early.
- Keep the hands connected to the rear shoulder.
- Organize the rear elbow.
- Turn the barrel into the hitting slot.
- Avoid pushing the hands directly toward the pitcher.
- Remain adjustable against fastballs, breaking balls, and changeups.

### Setup
1. Use a basketball or another large, lightweight ball.
2. Begin in a comfortable batting stance.
3. Hold the ball near the rear shoulder and hand area.
4. Keep the rear elbow comfortably elevated rather than collapsed against the side.
5. Make sure the surrounding area is clear.
6. Perform the first repetitions slowly and without a bat.

The basketball should help organize the starting position. Do not squeeze it excessively or force the arms into an uncomfortable position.

## Step-by-Step Explanation on Roll Drill
This drill helps you to have a good separation when you are batting. It allows the batter to move their lower body while the upper body remains loaded.
### 1. Load the Rear Side
Begin by loading into the rear hip and engaging the rear shoulder blade.

The hands, rear elbow, and rear shoulder should feel connected as one organized unit. Avoid allowing the hands to drift away from the shoulder or the rear elbow to collapse prematurely.

The hitter should feel balanced and prepared to move forward.

### 2. Stride while Keeping the Hands Back
Begin the stride toward the pitcher while keeping the basketball near the rear shoulder.

The lower body moves forward, but the hands do not travel forward with it. This creates separation between the lower and upper body.

Do not pull the hands farther behind the body. Simply keep them organized while the lower body gains ground.

### 3. Land without committing the hands
When the front foot lands:
- The hitter should remain balanced.
- The hands should still be near the rear shoulder.
- The rear elbow should remain organized.
- The rear hip should still feel loaded.
- The hitter should still be capable of delaying or stopping the swing.

Landing does not mean the swing has already started. It places the hitter in an adjustable launch position.

### 4. Begin the Barrel Turn
After landing and deciding to swing, the lower body and torso begin rotating. The rear elbow works naturally down and around as the barrel turns behind the hitter and enters the hitting slot.

This movement is the “roll.”

The hitter should feel the barrel beginning to turn while the hands remain connected near the rear shoulder for a brief moment.

Do not force the rear elbow against the body. It should work into the slot as part of the body’s rotation rather than being pulled down independently.

### 5. Avoid pushing the ball forward
A hand push occurs when the hands move directly toward the pitcher before the barrel has turned into the hitting zone.

This creates a steep, narrow bat path resembling the letter “V.” The bat cuts across the pitch’s path and remains in the hitting zone for only a short time.

A smooth barrel turn creates a longer path resembling the curve of a Nike swoosh. The barrel enters the pitch’s plane earlier, travels through the contact area, and gives the hitter more room for timing adjustments.

A useful cue is:

> Turn the barrel first; do not shove the hands toward the ball.

### 6. Use the basketball as feedback
The basketball makes incorrect movement easier to recognize.

The hitter may be pushing or disconnecting if:
- The ball moves away from the rear shoulder during the stride.
- The hands travel toward the pitcher before landing.
- The rear elbow collapses too early.
- The arms move without the torso.
- The hitter loses balance while trying to rotate.

If any of these problems occur, slow down and repeat the movement. Focus on landing with the basketball organized near the rear shoulder before beginning the turn.

### 7. Transfer the feeling to the bat
After several controlled basketball repetitions, repeat the sequence with a bat:
1. Load into the rear hip.
2. Engage the rear shoulder blade.
3. Stride while keeping the hands near the rear shoulder.
4. Land in a balanced, adjustable position.
5. Begin rotating with the lower body and torso.
6. Allow the rear elbow and barrel to work into the slot.
7. Let the hands follow the barrel turn.
8. Continue through a long, smooth hitting zone.

Begin with dry swings. Progress to tee work and controlled front toss only after the movement becomes comfortable and repeatable.
## Importance of the Role
By avoiding the roll and creating a good separation makes batter to adjust to a breaking ball (slider, curve, sweeper) or off speed pitch (change up, cutter, sinker, spliter) 

Imagine a hitter expecting a fastball. The pitcher throws a changeup instead.

A hitter who creates separation lands with the hands still connected near the rear shoulder. Because the hands have not fired prematurely, the hitter has more time to recognize the changeup.

The hitter can delay the final barrel turn, remain balanced, and deliver the bat on the correct plane.

This is adjustability: the body is prepared to swing, but the hands have not committed too early.

## Goal of the Drill
The goal is to develop a repeatable launch position in which the hitter:
- Creates separation between the lower and upper body.
- Lands with the hands close to the back shoulder.
- Keeps the back elbow from collapsing prematurely.
- Preserves time to recognize and adjust to different pitches.
- Starts the swing with a roll instead of a hand push.
- Gets the barrel on plane early and keeps it through the hitting zone longer.
