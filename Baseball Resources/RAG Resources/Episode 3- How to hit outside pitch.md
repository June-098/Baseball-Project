
## Hitting the Outside Pitch
Back in the day and still being taught incorrectly today is you've seen the three ball drill where they put one ball out in front of the inside corner. The ball down the middle on the front part of the plate and then everybody's being taught to hit the outside pitch deep in the zone.

In reality the professional player hits every single ball at the same contact point. The approach to hit outside pitch uses a more consistent reference point. Instead of allowing the outside pitch to travel excessively deep, the hitter aims to make contact near the plane of the front foot—the position of the front foot after landing.

The hitter does not need three completely different swings. The general contact area remains consistent, while the barrel’s direction changes according to the pitch location

## Barrel Direction on an Outside Pitch

The barrel should work from behind the baseball, forward through the hitting zone, and slightly upward through contact. This is sometimes described as a south-to-north or Ferris-wheel movement.

The hitter should avoid swinging horizontally around the body. That east-to-west movement causes the barrel to cut across the plate and leave the hitting zone too quickly.

To cover the outside pitch:
1. Land in a balanced position.
2. Keep the hands connected to the body.
3. Allow the back shoulder to work toward the pitch.
4. Use the legs, back knee, and back hip to help the body rotate.
5. Turn the barrel behind the ball.
6. Continue through the outside part of the plate.
7. Drive the ball toward the middle or opposite-field gap.

The hitter should not reach for the ball using only the arms. The lower body and torso help move the barrel into position while the hitter remains balanced.

## How to we improve hitting outside ball?
Use the Bosu ball drill and front-toss progression
## Bosu Ball Drill:
The BOSU Ball Drill helps the hitter feel how the back knee, back hip, and rear shoulder work together to reach the outside pitch. For a reference, watch this video (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=bMPY5FJup6g) at timestap 2:47 if you are confused with the writings. Source: Gradum Gswing
### Setup
1. Place a BOSU trainer securely between the hitter’s legs as a movement guide. The hitter should not stand on it.
2. Set a batting tee on the low-outside part of the strike zone.
3. Make sure the hitter has enough space to swing safely.
4. Begin with slow, controlled repetitions.

### Execution
1. Take a normal batting stance around the BOSU trainer.
2. Load into the back hip while remaining balanced.
3. Move forward and land on the front foot.
4. Drive the back knee and back hip toward the hitting area.
5. Allow the back shoulder to work toward the outside pitch.
6. Turn the barrel behind the ball and into the pitch’s path.
7. Continue through the ball toward the middle or opposite side of the field.

The low-and-away fastball is one of the most difficult pitches to hit, especially when it has high velocity. The hitter must stay balanced and keep the barrel in the hitting zone for as long as possible.

## Front-Toss Progression
After practicing the movement with the BOSU trainer and tee, remove those training aids and repeat the same movement during controlled front toss.
1. Begin with low-and-away tosses.
2. Allow the hitter to recognize the moving ball.
3. Maintain the same back-knee, back-hip, and rear-shoulder movement.
4. Keep the barrel working through the outside part of the plate.
5. Progress to middle-away and high-away pitches.
6. Adjust the barrel angle to match the height of each pitch.

The goal is to transfer the movement from a stationary drill to a moving baseball.

## Common Mistakes
Please avoid having these mistakes to improve hitting outside pitches. 
Avoid the following mistakes:
- Allowing the outside pitch to travel excessively deep.
- Reaching for the ball with the arms.
- Pulling the front shoulder away from the pitch.
- Swinging horizontally around the body.
- Cutting across the ball.
- Using the same barrel angle for both high and low pitches.
- Losing balance while trying to cover the outside corner.
