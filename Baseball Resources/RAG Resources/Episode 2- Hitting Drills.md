## Mike Trout's Famous Hitting Drill
Mike Trout’s batting-practice routine demonstrates the importance of using the entire field. During his first round, he often focuses on driving the ball toward right-center field and right field. He then works through the middle of the field, occasionally driving a ball toward left-center to make sure he stays square to the pitcher and maintains his balance.

One quality that makes Trout an exceptional hitter is his ability to stay through the middle of the field against pitches in different locations. Whether the pitch is inside, down the middle, or outside, he can drive it toward left-center, center, or right-center with authority.

The goal for an amateur hitter should not be to copy Trout’s exact swing. Instead, learn from his approach: stay balanced, remain square to the pitcher, and develop a barrel path that allows you to use the entire field.

## What is Ferris Wheel swing or Ferris Wheel?
Many hitters are taught to swing the bat horizontally around their body. This is sometimes called an east-to-west or merry-go-round swing.

That type of movement can cause the barrel to cut across the hitting zone. For a right-handed hitter, the barrel may move too quickly toward the left side of the field after contact, resulting in pulled ground balls or sharply hooked foul balls.

A more effective approach is to let the barrel work from behind the body into the hitting zone and then forward through the baseball. This is described as a south-to-north or Ferris-wheel movement.

The Ferris-wheel description does not mean that the hitter should create a steep uppercut. It means the barrel turns into the pitch’s path and continues forward with a controlled upward direction.



## How do we create Ferris Wheel swing or hit it like professional or how do we improve the bat path using Ferris Wheel motion?

The Drop-Bat Drill teaches the hitter to let the barrel enter the hitting zone from behind the baseball instead of pushing the hands directly toward the pitch or cutting across it.

This drill is credited in the source material to hitting instructor Craig Wallenbach. Watch the video (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-Z5r9275-yM) at 3:10 time stamp. Source: Gradum Gswing

### Step-by-Step of Drop-Bat drill (Improving bat path or swinging like Ferris Wheel)
1. Have the hitter take a normal batting stance.
2. The coach or partner delivers an easy front toss.
3. As the ball is tossed, the hitter allows the barrel to settle into its working position while maintaining control of the bat.
4. The hitter then turns the barrel into the pitch’s path.
5. The barrel works from behind the ball, forward through the hitting zone, and slightly upward through contact.
6. Begin slowly and increase the speed only after the hitter can repeat the movement safely.

The hitter should not release or physically drop the bat. “Drop the bat” refers to allowing the barrel to settle into position before turning it through the hitting zone.
### Pros of Drop-bat Drill:
- Allow the ball to travel into a better contact area.
- Avoid rushing the hands toward the pitch.
- Turn the barrel into the hitting zone from behind the ball.
- Reduce pulled ground balls and hooked foul balls.
- Adjust to inside, middle, and outside pitches.
- Drive the baseball toward all parts of the field.
- Remain balanced and square to the pitcher.
