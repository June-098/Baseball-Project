## What is Top hand?
The top hand is your dominant hand. Top hand is really important when it comes to hitting, because the top hand should remain connected to the back shoulder, guide the barrel onto its track, and stay palm-up through the contact area. The coach describes this as:

> **“Connect, roll, and deliver the pizza.”**

The purpose is to get the barrel into the hitting zone early, keep it there longer, and prevent groundball rollovers, weak pop-ups, foul balls, and swings under breaking pitches.

## Two Incorrect Ways to Use the Top Hand

### Mistake 1: Trying to “get on top” of the ball

Some hitters are told to use the top hand like a hammer and drive it downward on top of the baseball.

This causes the top forearm to pronate too early. **Pronation** means the forearm rotates so the palm begins turning downward.

For a right-handed hitter, the right palm turns down prematurely. This makes the barrel travel steeply across the ball’s path.

Common results include:

- A short hitting zone.
- Topped groundballs.
- Pull-side rollovers.
- Topspin instead of useful carry.
- Difficulty covering a slider on the outside part of the plate.
- A barrel that enters and exits the zone too quickly.

The top hand should not hammer down through contact.

### Mistake 2: Turning the barrel by disconnecting the hands

Another mistake occurs when the hitter tries to turn the barrel by moving the top hand away from the back shoulder.

The hands and bat separate from the body, and the barrel leaves its intended track. The hitter may feel fast, but the barrel becomes difficult to control.

Common results include:

- Pop-ups.
- Foul balls.
- Swings and misses.
- Inconsistent contact.
- Difficulty adjusting to changing pitch speeds.
- A barrel that works around the ball instead of through it.

The hands should not fly away from the rear shoulder to create barrel speed.

## The Correct Top-Hand Movement

The correct sequence has three parts:

1. **Connect to the back shoulder.**
2. **Roll the forearm and barrel into position.**
3. **Deliver the pizza toward center field.**

The word “roll” does not mean rolling the wrist over the ball. It describes the top elbow and forearm helping the barrel turn into the hitting slot while the hand remains connected to the shoulder.

Through contact, the top forearm stays **supinated**. Supination means the palm remains facing upward—like a server carrying a pizza.
