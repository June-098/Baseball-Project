**Logics of Hitting Breaking Balls**

- **Visual Displacement:** Breaking balls, such as curveballs, sliders, and splitters, are inherently difficult because they are designed to move downward, which forces the hitter’s **eye vision** to drop.
- **Pitcher Neutralization:** The logic of successful hitting involves **"taking away" the pitcher's best weapon**. By learning to hit a pitcher's signature slider or curveball, a batter forces the pitcher to rely on secondary options, increasing the batter's chance of success.

**Hitting Mechanisms and Trajectory**

- **The Barrel Path Rule:** The most critical mechanical point is that the **barrel path must match the path of the breaking ball**.
- **Avoiding "Short" Swings:** Traditional advice like being "short to the ball" or "swinging down" is often counterproductive against breaking pitches because it prevents the barrel from staying on the ball's trajectory.
- **Body Direction:** For right-handed hitters, a common mechanical flaw is the body pulling toward the third-base side on a ball breaking away; if the body drifts, the barrel cannot stay on the pitch's line.
- **The "In and Out" Principle:** The source emphasizes that "the way you come in is the way you go out," meaning the initial entry angle of the bat into the zone determines the entire swing path.
