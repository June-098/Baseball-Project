The batting mechanism is a scientific methodology designed to answer the fundamental question of how to hit a ball as **accurately and strongly** as possible. This process follows a specific, invariant sequence known as the **Batting Kinetic Chain Sequence**.

1. The Trigger and Timing

The batting process begins with a **trigger**, which is the initial movement used to set the rhythm and catch the timing of the pitch.

- **Variations:** Different batters use different triggers, such as **wiggling the bat**, tapping a foot on the ground, or performing a **large leg kick**.
- **The Bottom-Up Flow:** Once the trigger starts, the movement flows from the ground up: starting with **knee rotation**, followed by **hip rotation**, and then the **rotation of the thorax and shoulders**.
- **Power Position:** This sequence leads the batter into the **"Launch Position"** or **"Power Position,"** where they are ready to strike.

2. The Execution (Kinetic Chain)

When a batter decides to swing—a calculation made within a split-second 0.03-second window—the following sequence occurs:

- **Landing:** The front leg lands on the ground.
- **Sequential Rotation:** The foot turns, followed by the knee, then the hip, and finally the upper body.
- **The Swing:** Only after this chain of rotation is complete does the **bat swing out**.

3. The Importance of the "Back Space"

Modern batting theory emphasizes that **80% of the mechanism** happens in the "back space"—the movements occurring before the bat actually touches the ball.

- **Weight Transfer and Torque:** Crucial elements include the **"Hip Hinge"** to maximize pelvic use and **"Upper-Lower Body Separation"** to create maximum torque through body twisting.
- **Example (Juan Soto):** Soto is noted for his ability to concentrate and hold power firmly on his **back leg axis** while maintaining a large upper-body twist.
- **Delivery Styles:** Energy is delivered either through **active forward weight transfer** (like Yordan Alvarez) or by **rotating on the back leg axis** (like Bryce Harper). The latter is often seen in prolific home run hitters like Aaron Judge and Shohei Ohtani.

4. Swing Trajectory and Impact

While past theories focused heavily on the moment of impact and the follow-through, modern mechanisms view these as mere **extensions of the flow** rather than the source of power.

- **The "Down Swing" Illusion:** Many players (like Alex Rodriguez or Jose Ramirez) describe a feeling of "swinging down," but in reality, the bat barrel follows a **circular, slightly upward path**. This feeling is created by the **elbow driving down** toward the body.
- **Shoulder Depth:** The trajectory is often determined by **shoulder depth**. A shallower shoulder entry (like Ronald Acuña Jr.) leads to lower launch angles and line drives, while a **deeper shoulder entry** (like Aaron Judge) allows the batter to "dig under" the ball, resulting in higher launch angles and more home runs.

Ultimately, while these technical sequences are vital, the source notes that **sensory feel and timing** remain the final, essential components that allow a batter to handle the extreme speeds of professional baseball.
