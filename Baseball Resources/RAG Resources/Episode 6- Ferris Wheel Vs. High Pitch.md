## Ferris Wheel is used to hit low pitch

the topic today is the Ferris Wheel Swing and the misconception of how the Ferris Wheel Swing works versus a high pitch swing.



The catcher sets up inside and low pitch, and the only way to get to the ball as a hitter is using Ferris Wheel motion.

And what we mean by Ferris Wheel is the back part of the swing starts in a Ferris Wheel motion. He gets into bat lag and from bat lag he snaps forward.

This is the misconception of the Ferris Wheel.

We don't wanna swing up.

This way we wanna start the swing to get on plane early behind us and then we wanna snap the barrel forward and that's what creates the Ferris Wheel look.

## Misconception of Ferris Wheel
Where major leaguers, minor leaguers, college athletes, high school athletes are making the mistake is they think that they have to have the same swing on every single pitch.

### High pitches or above Belly Button doesn't work
Because the pitches are high, There is no way that he is gonna try to Ferris Wheel this, because Ferris Wheel is going South to North. 


### How to overcome high pitches using plane level adjustment
The way to make adjustment from Ferris Wheel to hit high pitch is to take his back shoulder to the ball and flatten out the swing. Also, we always want to get our bats into the plane early. It's still starting to like a Ferris Wheel but then all he's gonna do is flatten out his barrel to get to this high pitch.

### One big mindset to have on high pitch:
Now, the high pitch is a pitch that we really don't wanna swing at.

This is a mistake and this is something that we really don't wanna even hit because we really can't do much with it.

So what happens is as soon as a pitcher throws you a high strike, set up right, if he sets it, throws it here and the hitter swings underneath it, I guarantee you the next thing the catcher's gonna do is just take him upstairs and take him upstairs.

As a hitter, we don't wanna swing at these pitches.

We wanna let go of the high pitch.
**We don't wanna swing anything above our midpoint line.**
