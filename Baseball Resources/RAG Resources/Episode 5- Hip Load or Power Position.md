## The hip load

### What is the Hip Load?
The hip load is the movement that places the hitter’s weight and tension into the rear hip before the swing. It is also called a hip hinge or getting into an athletic position to hit.

### Why is the hip load important?
A proper hip load gives the hitter two important benefits: balance and power.

Elite hitters load into the rear hip so they can remain balanced for a brief moment while tracking the pitch. This helps them control their timing instead of rushing forward.

The hip load also prepares the body to rotate forcefully. When the rear glute and hamstring are engaged, the hitter can use the lower body to create rotational power through the swing.

### How to get into hip load or athletic stance?
The glute and hamstring help stabilize and rotate the hip. To engage those muscles, the hitter should hinge at the hip rather than simply bending the knees.

Standing too tall and pushing the knees forward usually places more pressure on the quadriceps. This is called becoming quad-dominant. A small amount of knee bend is necessary, but bending only at the knees does not create an effective hip load.

Instead, move the hips backward while maintaining a slight knee bend. Imagine pulling the rear hip toward the dugout behind you.

The hitter’s chest may lean forward slightly, but the movement should come from the hips—not by rounding or bending through the lower back. Hitters such as Joey Votto and Mike Trout often appear slightly tilted forward because they are hinging at the hips and loading their posterior chain.


### Finding the Athletic Position
1. Begin in a comfortable batting stance.
2. Shift your balance into the rear leg.
3. Bend both knees slightly.
4. Move the hips backward as though you are closing a car door with your rear hip.
5. Keep the back in a strong, neutral position.
6. Hold the position briefly and feel the rear glute and hamstring engage.
7. Remain balanced without allowing the weight to move outside the rear foot.

This resembles the beginning of a Romanian deadlift, commonly called an RDL. The hips move backward while the knees remain slightly bent and the torso tilts forward as one strong unit.

Do not make the movement excessively deep. The purpose is to feel tension in the rear hip, glute, and hamstring while remaining comfortable and athletic.


### How to avoid not having any hinge or athletic stance feeling
A sway occurs when the hitter moves the entire body over or beyond the rear foot instead of loading into the rear hip.

When the hitter sways:
- The head and upper body drift backward.
- The weight moves to the outside of the rear foot.
- The front leg may kick upward.
- The hitter must rush forward to recover.
- Timing and balance become difficult to control.

The correct movement is smaller. Pull the rear hip backward into the hip socket while keeping the body balanced inside the rear foot.

Think about loading into the rear hip rather than leaning over the rear leg.

## Avoid Becoming Quad-Dominant
Common signs include:
- The hips remain tall.
- The chest remains completely upright.
- The rear knee moves sharply forward.
- The hitter does not feel tension in the rear glute or hamstring.
- The lower body pushes upward instead of rotating through the swing.

As a training cue, keep the rear knee from driving excessively over the toes. Allow the rear hip to move backward so the glute and hamstring can engage.

Do not force the knee into a rigid position. The objective is to create a comfortable hip hinge, not to eliminate all natural knee movement.
