## Mike Trout's Hitting Drill

I come, you know, we come stretch, hit BP, first round just stay right center, you know, while it's the right field.

And then just stay out the middle of the whole time.

A couple of times I try to hit the ball left center just to stay square with the pitcher and keep myself balanced.

One of the things that makes Mike Trout special is that he's able to stay in the middle of the field on all pitches.

Whether it's an outside pitch, middle pitch, or inside pitch, he can take the ball left center, center, right center, and he can take it with authority and power.

The way he does it is simple.

He doesn't work in the way that most people have been taught how to hit.

He does not work east to west on his swing, we call east to west.

Everybody's been taught that the bat operates this way.


## Barrel Work

**When in reality the bat operates in a south to north fashion, we call this the ferris wheel, which is now a term that's being used quite a bit.**

Coined in a couple of years ago, we want to work from back to front, south to north, north to south, depending where you're at.

**We don't want to work in a married-around fashion with our barrel in or east to west.**

So it's simple.

If I'm swinging east to west, I'll consume so much of time to get to the ball, because the pitch is coming to us in a straight line. And when I make contact, which has to be off my front foot, my barrel's going to, as a right-handed hitter, to the left field, part of the field, their base, left field, and I'm going to be hooking balls.

I want to be working south to north, back to front, where I can be making contact off my front foot, let the ball travel, and now I can go right-center, center, or left-center.

So that's hugely important on how the barrel works.

We don't want all our exit velocity speed going to the left side and hooking balls, especially on the inside part of the pitch.

### Barrel Work Drill:
One of the better drills that we can show you on how to do this consistently, and my child does this drill, does it consistently, is the drop-bat drill.

This is a great drill that we got from Craig Wallenbach in California.

I definitely want to give him credit, because he did come up with this drill.

You know, we personally think that the industry has to start giving credit where credit is due.

Too many guys steal drills from other instructors and don't give the credit where it's done.

We're going to have new drills, everybody's got new drills.


Drop-bat drill.

The drop-bat drill is a drill done on front toss, can't be done on tee, should be done on front toss.

The ball gets flipped to the hitter, he gets in his position, and as soon as we flip the ball, he's going to drop the bat in his hands.

So again, now once he gets that bat dropped in his hands, now he can go to the pitch and come underneath, south to north, not east to west, if it's middle.

There it is.

If it's outside, he can get to it.

### Pros of Drop-bat Drill:
And if you notice, he's got to let that ball travel, because the bat has been shortened also.

So A, it helps you let the ball travel to the hitting zone, which is ideal spotters out from front foot or in your front foot.

B, it allows you to take that ball right center and left center.

So it really forces you to have to come underneath and work south to north.

And now here's the outside pitch, and he can make good contact and take it to right center.

If you notice, he's staying in the middle of the field.

Great drill.
