## The hip load

### What is the hipload?

The first thing it does for us is give us balance

So when you see the best hitters in the big leagues, you'll see them load into a position where they're able to hold for a split second to help with their timing.

The second thing it gives us is it helps us get into an athletic stance.

What does that mean?

It means that we're getting into a position where we're able to violently rotate our body to generate power through our swing.

### How to get into hip load or athletic stance?

The primary rotators over our hip are the glute max and that posterior leg, that hamstring area.

In order to use that part of our leg, we need to be able to activate it.

How do we activate it?

A lot of hitters come in, and the first thing they do is they want to stand up real nice and tall.

Only problem with that is that if I'm standing really nice and tall, usually I'm creating more of a bend in my knees.

When I bend my knees, back up a little bit, when I bend my knees, I'm loading my quad.

I'm not loading my hip.

I'm not loading my hamstrings.

What happens there is if that glute and that hamstring is not loaded, we're not able to extend and pop our hip through our swing.

If our quads are loaded, all we're going to do is create more of an upward motion, not a rotational motion on that hip.

One of the things that players worry about a lot is, oh, I feel like I'm bending over.

When I really sit into my glute, when I sit into my glute, for example, you're looking on the screen, we've got Doi Vado, one of the best, and Mike Trout, arguably the best right now.

You're always going to see a shadow over their jersey.

You're going to see a shadow facing down from the lights.

That shows you clearly that they're in a somewhat of a bent over position.

One thing to make clear is that they're not bending over, hinging at the back.

They're hinging at the hip.

All they're doing is loading into their hip.

They're pulling their hip back towards the dugout.


### Steps to get into hinge stance or athletic stance.
Let's get into how we actually load our hip.

First thing we'll ask guys to do is just get in a regular comfortable stance.


We'll ask them to load it, to just balance on their rear leg, that leg that's getting loaded.

From there, I'm going to ask them to bend their knees slightly. From there, I'm going to ask them to crack their hips back. I just want them to hold it there. Find some balance in that hip.

The purpose of this drill is basically just to show them what it feels like for the glute and that hamstring to get fired up a little bit.

It's kind of similar to doing like an RDL, like the first movement of an RDL.

We're going to bend the knees.

We're going to pull our hips back just to here and that's basically our position.

We're getting into that glute and that hamstring.

From there, I'm going to have them hold it, find some balance and then we start working on the walk out.

### How to avoid not having any hinge or athletic stance feeling
First thing to avoid:
If they're feeling more of their quad, you're going to see their hips kind of stay tall.

Their chest is going to stay really tall and that knee is going to push forward over those toes.

If the knee is pushing forward over the toes, our hips are not loaded.

We want that knee to stay behind the toe and we want that hip to work back behind us.
Second thing to avoid:
That outside foot.

If it's slightly turned outward, we're going to have a tendency when we go to load into our back hip for this knee to bow out outside of our foot.

The second that happens, we're losing the angles of force that we want to create as we go forward.

If my knee bows out, as I go to create a positive movement, I'm going to get stuck in place.

I'm not truly gathered.

I'm not truly gaining ground.
I'm really getting stuck in place and creating more of a spinning reaction once I get into my swing.

So what we want to do is make sure that that back foot is either straight or slightly turned in.
